here's the thing: a closed-loop benchmark can politely hide your queueing problem. 🔥

let me explain 👇 kv memory math is mostly multiplication, which makes my first spreadsheet especially embarrassing.

for our fictional full-attention config:
- 32 layers
- 8 kv heads per layer
- head dim 128
- bf16 cache: 2 bytes per element

1) each token stores both k and v:
2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 131,072 bytes.

that's 128 kib per token. use the kv head count, NOT the 32 query heads in this config. gqa matters here.

2) an 8,192-token sequence therefore needs 1 gib of kv. that's its current cached length, including generated tokens, not just the input prompt.

3) a 40 gib kv pool holds 327,680 token slots. in the tidy case of independent, equally sized sequences, that's 40 sequences at 8,192 tokens each.

it isn't a promise of 40 requests forever. decoding grows them, block allocation rounds them, and shared prefixes can change physical occupancy. weights and workspaces also need memory outside that pool.

lesson: calculate bytes per token before arguing about batch size. the arithmetic is cheap; finding out during load testing is our premium option.

let me explain 👇 continuous batching isn't “batch size 32, but goated.”

our old loop effectively did this:
a) collect 32 requests
b) run until every sequence finishes
c) collect the next 32

with 31 short answers and 1 long answer, the tail of that batch was mostly empty seats. new requests waited outside while the gpu decoded for the last sequence.

the continuous version makes a scheduling decision each iteration. finished sequences leave; queued requests can enter as capacity permits. on a replay of 2,400 requests, that moved us from 790 to 1,160 output tok/s on the same gpu.

but admission isn't free. new requests need prefill, existing requests need decode, and both want time on the device. admitting every waiting prompt immediately gave us ugly gaps in ongoing streams.

we capped prefill work per iteration and gave up 4% of peak throughput to keep p99 inter-token latency under 55ms.

lesson: filling empty seats helps. making every passenger board during every bite of dinner does not.
